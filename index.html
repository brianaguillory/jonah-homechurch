The Book of Jonah

A 4-week devotional through running, repenting, obeying, and learning to want what God wants.

Week 1 · Jonah 1:1–10 The Call You Already Know
0 / 7 ▶
Jonah 1:1Today

"The word of the Lord came to Jonah." God speaks first. That's how it always starts.

Reflect
  • When has God's word come to you in a way you couldn't ignore — even if you tried?
  • What does it mean that God initiates? He comes to Jonah, not the other way around.
Pray
God, you are the one who speaks first. Thank you for not waiting until I was ready. Speak to me again today — I'm listening.
Jonah 1:2Day 2

God calls Jonah to Nineveh — the great, wicked city that Jonah's people feared and despised.

Reflect
  • Is there a "Nineveh" in your life — a person, place, or situation God is calling you toward that you'd rather avoid?
  • Why do you think God sends his people toward the hard places instead of around them?
Pray
God, show me the hard place you're calling me toward. Give me the courage not to flinch before I even start moving.
Jonah 1:3Day 3

Jonah gets up — and goes the opposite direction. He pays the fare and boards a ship to Tarshish.

Reflect
  • Where are you running right now — and are you spending real resources (time, money, energy) to fuel it?
  • What does your Tarshish look like — the destination you choose when you don't want to deal with what God is saying?
Pray
God, I know what you're asking. I've been packing bags instead of obeying. I'm turning around — even if it's scary.
Jonah 1:4–5aDay 4

God sends a great wind. The storm is so violent the sailors are terrified — and they're experienced sailors.

Reflect
  • Has God ever sent a "storm" that made it obvious you were going the wrong way? What was it?
  • Where in your life right now is there turbulence that might be more than coincidence?
Pray
God, don't let me be so stubborn that you have to send a hurricane. Open my eyes to the smaller signals before the storm gets worse.
Jonah 1:5b–6Day 5

The sailors pray. The captain finds Jonah asleep below deck. "Get up and call on your God!"

Reflect
  • Where are you 'asleep below deck' — numb or checked out — while chaos grows around you?
  • Have unbelievers ever called you to pray when you were the one who should have been leading in prayer?
Pray
Wake me up, God. I don't want to sleep through what you're doing. Make me someone who prays when it matters — not last.
Jonah 1:7–8Day 6

The sailors cast lots. The lot falls on Jonah. "Who are you? What have you done?"

Reflect
  • What happens when what's hidden about you gets exposed? How do you usually respond?
  • Is there something God is surfacing in your life right now that you've been hoping would stay buried?
Pray
God, you already see everything hidden. Search me. Bring it into the light — I'd rather face it now than let it keep causing damage.
Jonah 1:9–10Day 7

Jonah tells the truth: "I am a Hebrew, and I fear the Lord, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land." The sailors are terrified.

Reflect
  • There's a gap between what Jonah says he believes ("I fear the Lord") and what he's been doing. Where is that gap in your life?
  • What does it mean to fear a God who made the sea — and then try to run across that same sea?
Pray
God, close the gap between what I say I believe and how I actually live. I don't want my actions to make my theology look like a lie.
Week 2 · Jonah 1:11–2:10 Grace at the Bottom
0 / 7 ▶
Jonah 1:11–14Day 8

The sailors don't want to throw Jonah in. They row harder first. They beg God not to hold them guilty for an innocent man's blood.

Reflect
  • These pagan sailors show more moral wrestling than the prophet does. What does that challenge you to think about?
  • Where have you seen unexpected integrity or compassion from people you wouldn't expect it from?
Pray
God, keep me humble. Don't let me assume I'm the one with the right heart just because I know the right words. Make me genuinely good, not just officially religious.
Jonah 1:15–16Day 9

Jonah goes overboard. The sea goes calm. The sailors offer sacrifices and make vows to God — on the spot.

Reflect
  • God used Jonah's failure to bring a crew of sailors to worship. Have you ever watched God do something good through your mess?
  • What does it do to your pride to know God can work in spite of you, not just through you?
Pray
Lord, use my story — even the parts I'm ashamed of. Don't waste any of it. Let people find you even through what I've gotten wrong.
Jonah 1:17Day 10

God appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah. He was in the belly three days and three nights — not dead, just held.

Reflect
  • The fish isn't punishment — it's rescue. Where has God's discipline in your life actually been protection?
  • What "belly of the fish" moment have you been through — a hard, dark, enclosed season that turned out to be God holding you?
Pray
Father, even my hard seasons are your mercy. You didn't let me drown. Thank you for the fish — even the ones that felt like punishment.
Jonah 2:1–4Day 11

Inside the fish, Jonah finally prays. Rock bottom is where he remembers to look up.

Reflect
  • How long does it usually take you to pray when things go sideways — days, weeks, or are you still waiting?
  • What is it about rock bottom that makes prayer finally feel necessary instead of optional?
Pray
I called from my distress, and you heard me. Teach me to call sooner — before the fish, not inside it. I don't want to wait for rock bottom anymore.
Jonah 2:5–7Day 12

"The waters closed in over me... yet you brought up my life from the pit, O Lord my God."

Reflect
  • Jonah is praying out of past-tense faith — remembering what God has done even while still in the fish. Can you do that right now?
  • What has God already brought you up from that you need to remember today?
Pray
You brought up my life from the pit before. You are doing it again, even when I can't feel it yet. I choose to remember your faithfulness right now.
Jonah 2:8–9Day 13

"Those who cling to worthless idols forfeit the grace that could be theirs. But I will sacrifice to you with a voice of thanksgiving. Salvation belongs to the Lord."

Reflect
  • What worthless idols — things you cling to instead of God — are costing you grace right now?
  • Jonah worships before anything has changed externally. What would it look like to thank God today, even before the fish opens?
Pray
Salvation belongs to you — not my plan, not my performance, not my comfort. I let go of the things I've been clutching. You are enough.
Jonah 2:10Day 14

God speaks to the fish. The fish vomits Jonah onto dry land. Three words. The fish obeys.

Reflect
  • God delivered Jonah before Jonah had done anything he was sent to do. What does that say about why God delivers us?
  • What 'shore' is God depositing you on right now — and are you willing to get up and walk toward what's next?
Pray
You restore even the ones who ran. I'm on the shore. I'm getting up. Walk with me toward what you called me to — I'm not going back to the boat.
Week 3 · Jonah 3:1–10 The Second Chance
0 / 7 ▶
Jonah 3:1–2Day 15

"The word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time." God doesn't give up on his messenger, even after the fish.

Reflect
  • Where has God given you a second chance to do something you ran from the first time?
  • What does it mean to you personally that God comes back — that he doesn't replace Jonah with someone more obedient?
Pray
Thank you for the second word, Lord. Thank you for not moving on to someone else. I hear you again — and I'm moving this time.
Jonah 3:3–4Day 16

Jonah goes to Nineveh. He walks in one day's worth and preaches five words: "Forty days and Nineveh will be overthrown."

Reflect
  • Jonah's message is minimal — he doesn't even invite repentance, just announces judgment. And it works. How does that challenge your ideas about what God needs from you?
  • Where are you waiting to be more prepared, more eloquent, or more confident before you obey?
Pray
God, use my small obedience. I don't have to have it all together. Send me with what I have, and you provide the results I could never manufacture on my own.
Jonah 3:5Day 17

The people of Nineveh believe God. They fast, great and small alike. The whole city responds.

Reflect
  • Have you ever underestimated what God could do in a person or place? What happened when you were wrong?
  • Who in your life have you mentally written off as unreachable? What does Nineveh say to that?
Pray
God, break my categories. Expand my expectations of what you can do. Don't let me decide who's too far gone before you even get a chance.
Jonah 3:6–7Day 18

The king of Nineveh hears, takes off his robe, puts on sackcloth, and sits in ashes. Then he issues a decree for the whole city to fast and cry out to God.

Reflect
  • Repentance started with one man at the top and moved outward. Who has your humility influenced lately?
  • What would it look like for you to take off the "robe" — the status, the self-protection — and just be honest before God today?
Pray
I take off the robe. No performance, no pretending. Here I am — just as I am. Let my honesty before you be something that gives others permission to be honest too.
Jonah 3:8–9Day 19

"Who knows? God may turn and relent... so that we may not perish." The king doesn't demand mercy — he simply hopes for it.

Reflect
  • There's something honest and beautiful about "who knows?" — not demanding, just hoping. Where do you need that posture right now?
  • Do you approach God demanding your preferred outcome, or coming with open hands?
Pray
Who knows, God? You might. And I trust that you might be more merciful than I expect. I come with open hands, not a list of demands.
Jonah 3:10Day 20

God saw their turning. And he relented from the disaster he had said he would do.

Reflect
  • Does God's willingness to relent from judgment bother you or comfort you — and what does your answer reveal about your own heart?
  • Where do you need God's mercy right now? Can you actually receive it without guilt or conditions?
Pray
You are slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love. I need that today. So does everyone around me. Thank you for relenting — even for the people I didn't think deserved it.
Reflection — Jonah 3Day 21

Pause and pray over the whole chapter. One reluctant prophet. One city. The biggest revival in the Bible.

Reflect
  • Where has God been forming you in the past three weeks — through the running, the fish, or the second chance?
  • Is there a "Nineveh" — a person or community — you feel God prompting you to move toward with what you know about him?
Pray
Lord, I don't want to be the reason your message is delayed. Use me — imperfect, uncertain, still being formed. You'll handle the results. I'll handle the going.
Week 4 · Jonah 4:1–11 The Heart Behind the Obedience
0 / 7 ▶
Jonah 4:1–2Day 22

Jonah is furious. He tells God: "This is exactly why I ran. I knew you'd do this — you're too gracious."

Reflect
  • Jonah obeyed — but his heart was never in it. Where in your life are you doing the right thing with completely the wrong motive?
  • Is there anyone whose repentance, blessing, or second chance quietly bothers you?
Pray
God, don't just change my behavior — change my heart. I don't want to obey with clenched fists. I want to want what you want.
Jonah 4:3–4Day 23

Jonah asks to die. "It is better for me to die than to live." God asks: "Is it right for you to be angry?"

Reflect
  • God doesn't scold Jonah — he asks a question. How does God's patience with Jonah's tantrum speak to you?
  • Where are you holding on to an anger or disappointment with God that you've never actually named out loud?
Pray
God, here's what I'm actually angry about. I know it might not be right. But I'd rather be honest with you than pretend it's not there. Help me work through it.
Jonah 4:5Day 24

Jonah leaves the city and sits down east of it, waiting to see what will happen. He makes himself a shelter and waits.

Reflect
  • Jonah is still hoping for judgment even after God showed mercy. Where are you holding out for an outcome God has already closed?
  • What would it look like to stop "waiting to see" and trust that God's decision is final — and good?
Pray
I'm done sitting east of the city, God. Help me stop waiting for the judgment you've already chosen not to send. Your mercy is the answer — even when it's not the one I wanted.
Jonah 4:6–7Day 25

God appoints a plant that gives Jonah shade. Jonah is very happy about the plant. Then God appoints a worm. The plant dies overnight.

Reflect
  • What small comfort are you more attached to than you realized — and what would happen if God took it away?
  • Why does losing a small thing sometimes hit harder than losing a big thing? What does that reveal?
Pray
God, you give and take away. Blessed be your name — even when the plant dies. Don't let my comfort become the thing I'm actually living for.
Jonah 4:8–9Day 26

A scorching east wind. The sun beats down. Jonah grows faint and again asks to die. "I am angry enough to die — over a plant."

Reflect
  • Jonah is more emotionally wrecked over a plant than over 120,000 people. Where are you more upset about your inconvenience than God's purposes for others?
  • What does discomfort reveal about what you're actually trusting in?
Pray
Lord, reorder what I care about. Don't let me spend my grief on plants when there are people. Use my discomfort to make me more like you.
Jonah 4:10–11Day 27

"You cared about the plant... Should I not care about Nineveh — that great city, with more than 120,000 people?" The book ends there. No answer from Jonah.

Reflect
  • The book ends with a question, not a resolution. What's your answer?
  • God cares about people who "don't know their right hand from their left." Who does that description make you think of in your own life?
Pray
Yes, Lord. You should care. And I want to care about what you care about — even the people I've been avoiding, even the city I didn't want to love. Make me someone who goes.
Reflection — The Whole BookDay 28

Jonah ran, repented (sort of), obeyed (reluctantly), and sulked (openly). God was patient through all of it. That's the whole story.

Reflect
  • Where do you see yourself most in Jonah — the running, the fish, the reluctant obedience, or the anger at mercy?
  • What one thing has God been saying to you through this book that you don't want to forget?
Pray
God, I don't want to be someone who preaches your mercy and privately resents it. Make my inside match my outside. I'm still being formed — and I'm grateful you haven't given up on that.
